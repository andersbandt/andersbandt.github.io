Roasted Beef Chuck Roast

← Beef

Prep Time: 1 hour  |  Cook Time: 2-3 hours  |  Serves: x people

Ingredients

  • Meat
  • Simple marine, herbs and spices (optional)
  • Salt and pepper

Instructions

  • Begin by taking the chuck roast out of the refrigerator at least an hour before cooking to bring it to room temperature. This ensures even cooking throughout. Season the roast generously with salt and pepper, or your preferred dry rub, allowing the flavors to penetrate the meat. For an extra layer of flavor, consider a simple marinade with herbs and spices, but this step is optional.
  • Preheat your oven to a low temperature, around 275°F to 300°F. Slow-roast the meat, maintaining a low and steady temperature until meat internal temperature is at desired level (see notes for more about cooking times and doneness)
  • Once the roast reaches your desired doneness, remove it from the oven and let it rest for 15-20 minutes. This resting period allows the juices to redistribute, ensuring a moist and tender cut. After resting, slice the roast against the grain to maximize tenderness. Serve with your choice of sides, such as roasted vegetables, mashed potatoes, or a rich gravy made from the pan drippings.

Notes

The cooking time will vary depending on the size of the roast, but a good rule of thumb is 1-2 hours per pound (or 2.2-4.5 hours for a 4-pound roast).

Aim for an internal temperature of 125°F to 130°F (52°C to 54°C) for medium-rare. This temperature range ensures the meat remains juicy and flavorful. If you prefer your meat more well-done, target 135°F to 140°F (57°C to 60°C). Remember, the meat will continue to cook as it rests, so slightly undercooking is preferable to overcooking.

Wagyu chuck roast, a cut of meat renowned for its rich marbling and exceptional flavor, can be transformed through the art of marinating. Marinating is a process that not only tenderizes the meat but also infuses it with a burst of flavor, making it a versatile and delicious option for various culinary creations. When it comes to enhancing the taste of wagyu chuck roast, marinades like soy sauce and garlic are a perfect combination.

Soy sauce, a staple in Asian cuisine, adds a savory and slightly salty flavor to the meat. Its umami taste, derived from the fermentation process, creates a deep and rich base note. When combined with garlic, which contributes a sharp and pungent aroma, the marinade becomes a powerful flavor enhancer. Garlic, known for its ability to elevate dishes, provides a distinct and aromatic touch to the roast. Together, they create a harmonious blend of flavors that will tantalize the taste buds.

To begin the marinating process, simply combine soy sauce, minced garlic, and a pinch of sugar in a bowl. The sugar acts as a natural preservative and helps to balance the saltiness of the soy sauce. Adjust the quantities to your preference, ensuring a generous amount of garlic for a bold flavor. Place the wagyu chuck roast in a resealable bag or a large bowl, coating it evenly with the marinade. Allow the meat to soak in the flavorful liquid, ensuring every inch is covered.

For optimal results, let the roast marinate in the refrigerator for a minimum of 2 hours, or even overnight for more intense flavors. The longer it sits in the marinade, the more the flavors will penetrate the meat, resulting in a tender and mouth-watering dish. Remember, the key to a successful marinade is patience, as the process allows the flavors to meld and create a culinary masterpiece.

After marinating, you can choose to cook the wagyu chuck roast in various ways. Grilling or roasting will further enhance the meat's natural juices and create a crispy exterior. Alternatively, you can opt for a slow cooker, allowing the flavors to meld gently over low heat. The marinating technique ensures that the roast becomes incredibly tender and juicy, making it a centerpiece of any special meal.
