Introduction

Background

Hydroponics is a method of growing plants (usually crops or medicinal plants) without soil. Instead, nutrient-filled water is utilized in an artificial environment. A small amount of inert medium such as perlite, gravel, or other substrates is used to support the plants roots.

There are lots of reasons why one may choose to use hydroponics. One is that it's very water efficient. I became interested in it as there is lots of different variables for a control system to be involved, hence my project began.

Motivations

I decided to embark on this project after wanting to

  1. explore embedded systems more by designing my own PCB
  2. an interest in automating and regulating plant growth

The goal of the project is to create a controller for automatically regulating a hydroponic environment. This is accomplished through various inputs and outputs. The controller is implemented option a custom PCB, designed and built by me.

Capabilities

We need a system that can regulate a hydroponic plants environment. What are the components of a plant environment?

  • light
  • water
    • nutrient content
    • pH
    • aeration
  • temperature and humidity

Light is fairly easy. We want to be able to turn on and off the lights. Light intensity is another feature to control through dimming, but is not critical.

Water control is probably the most interesting part of the build. We essentially need to add nutrients in certain doses, and also regulate the pH.

pH regulation is most commonly done with "pH up and down" solution.

The actual administration of these liquids (nutrients and pH solution) is commonly done with peristaltic pumps. These are a type of positive displacement pump used for pumping a variety of fluids.

Temperature and humidity control is the last component. Really the only "dials" I was able to come up to turn with this is fan speed. I know some people use misters to control humidity, I haven't gotten into that yet.
